Building Creative Melbourne

How architecture, art and big ideas shaped our city

From the tiny artist garrets in the Nicholas Building to the Great Hall at National Gallery Victoria, we take an unforgettable tour through the creative world of Melbourne.

What you'll see

We start in City Square, in the middle of the CBD, and finish across the river in the Arts Precinct. This is the walk about why Melbourne's creative life looks the way it does: the artists working in cheap rooms above Swanston Street, the architects handed the big Arts Precinct commissions, and the ideas and arguments behind what got built.

Two hours, a lot of ground, and a few stories you won't hear elsewhere.

Who it suits

Anyone who likes art, buildings or Melbourne, in any combination. Visitors get a proper introduction to the Arts Precinct. Locals get the stories behind places they've been walking past for years.

Your guide is Nick Heydon, an architectural guide who runs all four Nick Walks Melbourne routes.

QuestionsAnswered by Nick

What is Building Creative Melbourne?

A two-hour walking tour with Nick Heydon about how architecture, art and big ideas shaped Melbourne. It starts in City Square and ends in the Arts Precinct, taking in the Nicholas Building, the NGV and the buildings around Hamer Hall. $50 per person.

What will I see on the walk?

The inside of the creative city: artist studios, galleries, concert halls and the streets between them. Expect Modernism and Brutalism alongside Victorian buildings, and plenty about the people who made them.

How long is it and how far do we walk?

Two hours at an easy pace, from the middle of the CBD across the river to St Kilda Road.

Where does the walk start?

In City Square, on the steps behind the entrance to Town Hall Station at the corner of Collins and Swanston Streets.
