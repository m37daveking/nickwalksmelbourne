Melbourne Architecture Walking Tours

Small-group walks across the CBD, the Arts Precinct and South Yarra, led by architectural guide Nick Heydon.

Architecture is human intention made visible. Every building is somebody's idea about how people should live, work or show off, and once you can read that, the city stops being a backdrop. That's what these walks are for.

Each one is built around people and ideas: the architects, developers, artists and eccentrics who made Melbourne, and the arguments they had along the way. Groups are small, the pace is unhurried, and the walks are written for locals as much as visitors.

Which walk?A quick guide

Which walk is right for you?

  • Got 45 minutes? City Lunchtime Walk. Top end of the CBD, $25, back at your desk within the hour.
  • Interested in art as much as buildings? Building Creative Melbourne. From the Nicholas Building to the NGV and the Arts Precinct.
  • Curious about those flats along the river? Howard Lawson. The architect and builder behind South Yarra's Hollywood-style apartments.
  • Want the wider South Yarra story and a train ride? Train & Walk. South Yarra Station to Anzac Station through the leafy streets.

What the walks have in common

Every route is led by Nick Heydon, in a small group, on foot. Prices run from $25 for the 45-minute lunchtime walk to $50 for the two-hour and 2.5-hour walks. You book a date on TryBooking and meet Nick at the point listed on each walk's page.

Nick's approach is the same on every walk: how to spot a Modernist masterpiece, what the stories hidden in the streets are, and the details you've probably walked past hundreds of times without noticing.

QuestionsAnswered by Nick

What are the best architecture walking tours in Melbourne?

That depends how long you've got. Nick Walks Melbourne runs four: the City Lunchtime Walk (45 minutes in the top end of the CBD), Building Creative Melbourne (two hours from the CBD to the Arts Precinct), Howard Lawson (about 2.5 hours in South Yarra) and Train & Walk (two hours from South Yarra Station to Anzac Station). All are small-group walks led by Nick Heydon.

What will I see on a Melbourne architecture tour?

It depends on the walk. Across the four: Australia's first skyscraper, Brutalist and Modernist landmarks, the NGV and Hamer Hall, Spanish Mission and Moderne flats along the Yarra, Arts and Crafts houses, work by Robin Boyd and Peter McIntyre, and some of the newest buildings in the city. Every walk also covers the people and ideas behind what you're looking at.

How long are Nick Walks Melbourne tours?

Between 45 minutes and 2.5 hours. The City Lunchtime Walk is 45 minutes and $25. Building Creative Melbourne and Train & Walk are two hours and $50. Howard Lawson is about 2.5 hours and $50.

Are the walks suitable for Melbourne locals as well as visitors?

Yes. They were written for locals in the first place: the whole idea is to notice the things you've walked past hundreds of times. Visitors get a proper introduction to the city from someone who lives in it.

Can I book a private or corporate Melbourne walking tour?

Yes. Any of the walks can be run privately for a workplace, club or group of friends. Email Nick with the walk you'd like, your preferred dates and the size of the group.

How do I book?

Each walk has its own TryBooking page with dates and times. Pick a date, book, and turn up at the meeting point listed on the walk's page. Gift certificates are available too.
